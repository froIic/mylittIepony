## delete driven development

frolic proudly operates under delete-driven development (ddd), a proprietary development methodology created internally to address the limitations of conventional project management. after years of innovation in software project management produced kanban boards, sprint planning, jira and alternatives, burndown charts, velocity tracking, and several exciting new ways to move rectangles between columns, i developed an alternative: delete-driven development. ddd is based on the observation that i do not particularly care about what i have already done. i care about how much shit is left.

there are two possible task states:

exists: needs to be done

does not exist: done

when a task is completed, it is deleted. this offers several advantages over traditional project management. the backlog gets physically smaller, progress can be determined by looking at how much stuff is still there, and no engineering resources are wasted dragging a rectangle from “in progress” to “done.” ddd also eliminates the psychological burden of staring at 70 completed tasks while trying to find the four that still matter. completed work instead takes on a revolutionary new form known as “the software.”

historical design decisions and documentation can obviously be kept elsewhere. delete-driven development concerns itself exclusively with actionable work. the methodology has one primary performance indicator: how much shit is left? the long-term target is zero. at zero, either the project is finished or you have made a catastrophic administration error. but technically both outcomes successfully clear the backlog. frolic intends to continue using delete-driven development until a superior methodology is discovered or this becomes somebody else’s problem. the methodology remains under active development, insofar as i keep deleting things.

enterprise adoption is currently discouraged for reasons that should be obvious. ddd is currently recommended exclusively for organizations with highly advanced operational structures, such as one person. frolic accepts no responsibility for anyone attempting to implement this methodology in an actual workplace. ddd has achieved a 100% adoption rate among its creator. frolic remains the current industry leader in delete-driven development, largely due to a lack of competition.
